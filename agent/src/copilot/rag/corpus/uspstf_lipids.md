## Who should be offered a statin

For primary prevention in adults aged 40 to 75 who have one or more
cardiovascular risk factors (such as dyslipidemia, diabetes,
hypertension, or smoking), the USPSTF recommends starting a statin when
the estimated 10-year risk of a cardiovascular event is 10% or higher;
this is a B recommendation. For the same adults whose estimated 10-year
risk is 7.5% to less than 10%, clinicians may selectively offer a statin
because the net benefit is smaller; this is a C recommendation. The
distinction is driven by the size of the expected absolute benefit.
(Paraphrased from the 2022 USPSTF statin recommendation.)

## Estimating cardiovascular risk

The recommendation is built on estimating a person's 10-year risk of a
first cardiovascular event, typically with the pooled cohort equations,
combined with the presence of specific risk factors. Because the
absolute benefit of a statin scales with baseline risk, the USPSTF
frames the risk estimate as the pivotal input to the decision rather
than the lipid value in isolation. Clinicians are encouraged to discuss
that these equations estimate probability at the population level and
carry uncertainty for an individual. (Paraphrased from the 2022 USPSTF
statin recommendation.)

## Adults 76 years and older

For adults 76 years and older with no history of cardiovascular disease,
the USPSTF concluded that the current evidence is insufficient to weigh
the balance of benefits and harms of initiating a statin for primary
prevention; this is an I statement. It is a statement about the limits
of the evidence, not a recommendation against treatment, so decisions in
this group rest on clinical judgment and individual circumstances.
(Paraphrased from the 2022 USPSTF statin recommendation.)

## Shared decision making and intensity

The USPSTF emphasizes that starting a statin for primary prevention
should follow a shared decision-making conversation covering the
person's risk, the expected benefit, potential harms, and their own
preferences. Most of the trial evidence supporting the recommendation
used moderate-intensity statin therapy, so that intensity is a
reasonable default for primary prevention in the eligible age range.
(Paraphrased from the 2022 USPSTF statin recommendation.)
