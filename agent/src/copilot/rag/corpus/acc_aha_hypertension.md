## Blood pressure categories

The 2017 ACC/AHA guideline defines blood pressure in four categories
using the average of two or more properly measured readings on two or
more occasions. Normal is a systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80
mm Hg. Elevated is a systolic of 120 to 129 with a diastolic below 80.
Stage 1 hypertension is a systolic of 130 to 139 or a diastolic of 80
to 89. Stage 2 hypertension is a systolic of 140 or higher or a
diastolic of 90 or higher. Lowering the stage 1 threshold to 130/80
reclassified more adults as hypertensive, chiefly to prompt earlier
lifestyle intervention. (Paraphrased from the 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood
Pressure Guideline.)

## Treatment thresholds and blood pressure goals

The guideline ties the decision to start medication to overall
cardiovascular risk, not blood pressure alone. Antihypertensive drug
therapy is recommended for adults with stage 1 hypertension who also
have clinical cardiovascular disease or an estimated 10-year
atherosclerotic risk of 10% or higher, and for essentially all adults
with stage 2 hypertension. A blood pressure target of less than 130/80
mm Hg is recommended for treated adults, including those with diabetes
or chronic kidney disease. (Paraphrased from the 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood
Pressure Guideline.)

## Lifestyle modification as foundational therapy

Nonpharmacologic measures are recommended for everyone with elevated
blood pressure or hypertension and are the sole initial therapy for
lower-risk stage 1 patients. The guideline highlights weight loss for
those who are overweight, a heart-healthy dietary pattern such as the
DASH diet, dietary sodium reduction, increased dietary potassium
through food, regular aerobic physical activity, and moderation of
alcohol intake. Each measure produces a modest but additive reduction in
systolic pressure. (Paraphrased from the 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood
Pressure Guideline.)

## First-line pharmacologic therapy

When medication is indicated, first-line agents are a thiazide diuretic,
a calcium channel blocker, or an ACE inhibitor or angiotensin receptor
blocker; ACE inhibitors and ARBs are not combined with one another. For
adults with stage 2 hypertension whose pressure is well above goal,
starting with two first-line agents from different classes is
reasonable. Agent selection is individualized for comorbidities such as
chronic kidney disease, in which a renin-angiotensin system blocker is
preferred. (Paraphrased from the 2017 ACC/AHA High Blood Pressure
Guideline.)
