## Glycemic targets and A1C goals

For many nonpregnant adults an A1C goal below 7% is reasonable, because
lowering A1C toward that level reduces the risk of microvascular
complications. The ADA frames this as an individualized decision: a
tighter goal (for example under 6.5%) can be pursued when it is
achievable without meaningful hypoglycemia or other harm, while a more
relaxed goal (such as under 8%) is appropriate for people with limited
life expectancy, advanced complications, extensive comorbidity, or a
history of severe hypoglycemia. The guidance emphasizes weighing the
benefit of lower glucose against the burden and risk of the regimen for
the individual patient. (Paraphrased from ADA Standards of Care 2025,
Glycemic Targets.)

## Screening and diagnosis of type 2 diabetes

Diabetes can be diagnosed by any of several thresholds: an A1C of 6.5%
or higher, a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL or higher, a 2-hour
plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher during a 75-g oral glucose
tolerance test, or a random glucose of 200 mg/dL or higher in someone
with classic symptoms. In the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia, an
abnormal result should be confirmed by repeat testing. The ADA
recommends screening asymptomatic adults who are overweight or obese and
have one or more additional risk factors, and screening all adults
beginning at age 35. (Paraphrased from ADA Standards of Care 2025,
Classification and Diagnosis.)

## Cardiovascular and kidney protection with newer agents

For people with type 2 diabetes who have established atherosclerotic
cardiovascular disease, heart failure, or chronic kidney disease, the
ADA recommends choosing glucose-lowering therapy that provides
cardiorenal benefit independent of its glucose effect. An SGLT2
inhibitor with proven benefit is favored when heart failure or chronic
kidney disease predominates, and a GLP-1 receptor agonist or SGLT2
inhibitor with proven benefit is favored to reduce atherosclerotic
cardiovascular risk. These choices are recommended regardless of
baseline A1C or metformin use, reflecting the shift toward
organ-protective therapy selection. (Paraphrased from ADA Standards of
Care 2025, Cardiovascular Disease and Risk Management.)

## Statin therapy for patients with diabetes

The ADA recommends statin therapy layered onto lifestyle change based on
age and cardiovascular risk rather than on LDL cholesterol level alone.
For adults with diabetes aged 40 to 75 without established
cardiovascular disease, moderate-intensity statin therapy is
recommended; high-intensity statin therapy is recommended when
additional cardiovascular risk factors are present or 10-year risk is
elevated. For those with established atherosclerotic cardiovascular
disease, high-intensity statin therapy is recommended with the goal of a
substantial reduction in LDL cholesterol. (Paraphrased from ADA
Standards of Care 2025, Cardiovascular Disease and Risk Management.)
